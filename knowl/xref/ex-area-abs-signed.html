Example 4.7.10.

Evaluate the integral below by interpreting it in terms of areas. In other words, sketch the graph of the integrand and compute the integral using geometry.
🔗
\begin{equation*} \displaystyle \int_{-1}^{3} |3x-6|\,dx \end{equation*}
Solution.
First let’s sketch the function \(f(x)=|3x-6|\text{.}\) These absolute value functions are V-shaped, and the vertex occurs where the expression inside the absolute value is zero.
🔗
Setting \(3x-6=0\) gives \(x=2\text{.}\) So the vertex is at the point \((2,0)\text{.}\)
🔗
We can also plot the endpoints of the interval: \(f(-1)=|3(-1)-6|=9\) and \(f(3)=|3(3)-6|=3\text{.}\) and connect these points with straight lines to get the graph of \(f(x)\text{.}\)
🔗
We can see from the graph that the region under the function forms 2 triangles both above the \(x\)-axis. The area of the triangle on the left is:
🔗
\begin{equation*} \text{Area}_1 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \text{base} \cdot \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 3 \cdot 9 = \frac{27}{2}. \end{equation*}
The area of the triangle on the right is:
🔗
\begin{equation*} \text{Area}_2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \text{base} \cdot \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1 \cdot 3 = \frac{3}{2}. \end{equation*}
Therefore, the value of the integral is the sum of these two areas:
🔗
\begin{equation*} \int_{-1}^{3} |3x-6|\,dx = \text{Area}_1 + \text{Area}_2 = \frac{27}{2} + \frac{3}{2} = 15. \end{equation*}
