Example 4.6.1.

(a)
Find the derivative of \(F(x) = \sin(x^2)\)
🔗
Solution.
By the Chain Rule, \(F'(x) = \cos(x^2)\cdot 2x\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗
(b)
Use you answer from (a) to help evaluate \(\displaystyle \int x\cos(x^2)\,dx\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Since \(F'(x) = \cos(x^2)\cdot 2x\text{,}\) we have
🔗
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} \displaystyle \int x\cos(x^2)\,dx \amp = \displaystyle \int \tfrac{1}{2}(2x)\cos(x^2)\,dx \\ \amp = \tfrac12\sin(x^2)+C \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
We can check this by differentiating: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\big(\tfrac12\sin(x^2)+C\big)=\tfrac12\cos(x^2)\cdot 2x=x\cos(x^2)\text{.}\)
